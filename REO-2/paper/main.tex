\begin{abstract}
Earth observation data is natively a time series of the same location, yet most
self-supervised objectives for satellite imagery treat acquisitions independently.
Predicting the \emph{future} latent state of a location from its past should be the
better-matched objective. We show that a direct port of the JEPA objective to
Sentinel-2 time series \textbf{collapses}: because consecutive acquisitions of a parcel are
near-identical, future-latent prediction is solvable by emitting a constant
(loss $\to 0$, per-dimension std $0.04$, effective rank $2.4$ of $512$).
Adding variance--covariance regularization on the context embedding removes the
degenerate solution. With it, causal temporal prediction outperforms spatial masking
by $+6.0$ mIoU on PASTIS crop segmentation under a frozen probe
($22.3$ vs.\ $16.2$, paired $t$-test $p=0.041$, 3 seeds), and the margin widens from
$+37\%$ at full supervision to $+100\%$ at $1\%$ labels. Probing frozen spatial features
shows the objective makes the representation phenology-aware: acquisition month decodes at
$61.3\%$ vs.\ $46.3\%$ (chance $8.3\%$).
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We identify and measure a failure mode specific to satellite image time series:
        temporal redundancy makes latent future-prediction degenerate, and we
        quantify the collapse via effective rank and per-dimension variance.
  \item Under a matched-backbone, matched-budget protocol, causal temporal prediction
        outperforms spatial masking and reconstruction/contrastive baselines on PASTIS,
        with the advantage growing as labels shrink.
  \item We give direct evidence for the mechanism: the temporal objective makes
        \emph{single-frame} spatial features season-aware, which is the crop-discriminative signal.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Given $X \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times 10 \times 128 \times 128}$ with acquisition days
$d \in [1,366]^{T}$, the encoder maps each frame to $N{=}256$ tokens of width $D{=}512$
($P{=}8$ patches). A temporal transformer aggregates frames $1{:}t$ causally; a narrow
predictor ($D_p{=}384$) maps the context to the EMA-target embedding of frame $t{+}\Delta$.
Day-of-year enters only as a periodic encoding; chronological order is the acquisition index.
